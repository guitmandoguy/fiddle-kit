Note by note

One row per note, highest first. Dots are each time you played it, the dark tick is the median, and the dashed tick is the just-intonation target in this key.

Tempo

Dots are local tempo in 6-second windows (noisy, ±3–5%); the line is their trend. Diamonds are repeats of the main phrase, the most reliable numbers.

Double stops

One row per double stop. Dots show how many cents the interval was wide (+) or narrow (−) of equal temperament; the dashed tick is the pure, beatless interval.

      Each note's pitch is read from its fundamental and first two overtones together, so a body resonance or sympathetic ringing can't drag the reading. A note counts as in tune if it is within 10¢ of equal temperament or of the just interval in the key. It also counts when it rings with your open strings (a pure 3rd, 4th, 5th or 6th against one), using the open strings as you tuned them in that take, or your last string check if it still matches. If that check found your strings wobbling more than usual under the bow, the 10¢ widens a little.

      A steady drone to play against. Fifths and octaves are tuned pure, so they lock with your open strings. On iPhone it keeps playing with the screen locked (from your GitHub link).

      Am I in tune?

      Play against the drone and see each note in cents. A note counts as in tune if it hits equal temperament or the pure interval against the drone. Headphones work best; through the speaker it ignores the drone itself and only reads you when you're playing.
