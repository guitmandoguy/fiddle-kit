Note by note

One row per note, highest first. Dots are each time you played it, the dark tick is the median, and the dashed tick is the just-intonation target in this key.

Tempo

Dots are local tempo in 6-second windows (noisy, ±3–5%); the line is their trend. Diamonds are repeats of the main phrase, the most reliable numbers.

Double stops

One row per double stop. Dots show how many cents the interval was wide (+) or narrow (−) of equal temperament; the dashed tick is the pure, beatless interval.

      A note counts as in tune if it is within 10¢ of equal temperament or of the just interval in the key. If it hits neither, it still counts when you place it the same way every time; it is flagged when it strays from your usual spot. Notes shorter than 0.15 s aren't graded. This measures pitch and timing only, not tone or feel. Double stops are read as both notes plus the interval between them; octave double stops read as one note. Band mixes aren't reliable.
